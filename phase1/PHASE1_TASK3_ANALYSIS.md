# Phase 1 Task 3 — escalation benchmark analysis

**Run:** 255 runs (17 cells × 5 conditions × N=3), one pass, no re-runs, no
tuning. Raw data: `phase1/results/task3/master_log.json` (gitignored — this
document plus the summary tables below are the reproducible record; re-run
`python phase1/task3_runner.py` to regenerate the raw dump). Generated by
`phase1/task3_runner.py`, which reuses `stitch_runner.run_one_condition()`
and `escalation_runner.run_one_replicate()` completely unmodified.

**Read this document as a mixed result, not a verdict for `sem_escalate`.**
The single most important finding is in §1: most of the pre-registered run
set doesn't test what it was built to test once you actually sample it
three times instead of once.

---

## §1. The premise didn't replicate — report this first

The harmful/enabling cell labels were built from N=1 historical data
(`phase0/evidence/combined_results.csv`). Task 3 ran fresh `baseline` at
N=3 on the same 13 cells. The property each label depends on — "does
`baseline` actually pass at iteration 1 (trivial) or ≥2 (real-test)?
does it actually fail (enabling)?" — mostly doesn't hold up:

| cell | original label basis (N=1) | fresh baseline, N=3 | replicates? |
|---|---|---|---|
| full_adder/deepseek | PASS\|1 (trivial) | [1,1,1] | yes |
| gray_counter_4bit/llama3.1 | PASS\|1 (trivial) | [1,1,1] | yes |
| sync_fifo_8/llama3.1 | PASS\|1 (trivial) | [1,1,1] | yes |
| ripple_carry_adder/deepseek | PASS\|1 (trivial) | [1,2,1] | mostly |
| sync_fifo_8/deepseek | PASS\|1 (trivial) | **[2,2,2]** | **no** |
| counter_4bit/deepseek | PASS\|2 (real-test) | [2,2,2] | **yes — the only real-test cell that holds** |
| full_adder/llama3.1 | PASS\|2 (real-test) | **[1,1,1]** | **no** |
| pipeline_mult_4x4/deepseek | PASS\|5 (real-test) | **[1,1,1]** | **no** |
| simple_cpu_alu/llama3.1 | PASS\|5 (real-test) | **[1,1,1]** | **no** |
| pwm_generator/deepseek | PASS\|4 (required for "harmful") | **[F6,F6,F6]** | **no — baseline now fails outright** |
| alu_8bit/deepseek (enabling) | baseline FAIL | **[1,1,1] PASS** | **no** |
| alu_8bit/gemma3:4b (enabling) | baseline FAIL | **[3,3,3] PASS** | **no** |
| spi_master_8bit/gemma3:4b (enabling) | baseline FAIL | [F6,F6,F6] | yes |

**4 of 5 "real-test" cells and 2 of 3 "enabling" cells do not reproduce the
baseline behavior their label was built on.** This is the exact confound
phase 0 already documented (§2a / §5b.1 of `phase0/evidence/phase0_illustration_hunt_findings.md`,
"N=1 makes the entire result unfalsifiable") showing up concretely at
production scale. Practically, it means only a small subset of the 17
cells actually exercise the escalation mechanism in a way worth reading
anything into — §2 covers exactly those.

## §2. Mixed pass-rate result on the cells that actually replicate — four results, equal weight

Everything else in the harmful/enabling sets tied trivially across all 5
conditions (baseline passed instantly, escalation structurally could not
fire, nothing to compare). These four cells are where there is a real
signal, and they point in different directions. None is presented as more
important than the others.

**Win — `pwm_generator`/deepseek-coder:6.7b** (baseline collapses to 0/3 in
fresh sampling — the hardest cell in the set):

| condition | pass | iters |
|---|---|---|
| baseline | 0/3 | [F6,F6,F6] |
| sem_adv | 1/3 | [2,F6,F6] |
| **sem_escalate** | **2/3** | [F8,2,2] |
| sem_escalate_restart | 1/3 | [2,F8,F8] |

`sem_escalate` beats both `baseline` and unconditional `sem_adv` here.
`sem_escalate_restart` ties `sem_adv` — one replicate reached and actually
fired a restart at iteration 7 (not preempted) and still failed.

**Structural win — `ripple_carry_adder`/deepseek-coder:6.7b:**

| condition | pass | iters |
|---|---|---|
| baseline | 3/3 | [1,2,1] |
| **sem_adv** | **0/3** | [F6,F6,F6] |
| sem_escalate | 3/3 | [1,2,1] |
| sem_escalate_restart | 3/3 | [2,2,2] |

Escalation never fires here — both escalation conditions match `baseline`
because the model never gets stuck long enough to escalate. Unconditional
`sem_adv` (tools on every iteration, from iteration 1) gets stuck for all
6 iterations in every replicate. The win is `sem_escalate` correctly
staying out of the way, not anything it did — the tools were actively
hurting this cell, and escalation's whole design point is not applying
them until there's evidence they're needed.

**Cost, no pass/fail change — `counter_4bit`/deepseek-coder:6.7b:**

| condition | pass | iters | stuck_fires |
|---|---|---|---|
| baseline | 3/3 | [2,2,2] | [0,0,0] |
| sem_adv | 3/3 | [2,2,2] | [0,0,0] |
| sem_escalate | 3/3 | **[2,2,8]** | [0,0,2] |
| sem_escalate_restart | 3/3 | [2,2,2] | [0,0,0] |

`sem_escalate` still passes all 3 replicates, but one escalates and burns
6 extra iterations before succeeding. This is the cost side showing up
even where escalation doesn't lose — worth weighing against the wins
above, not omitted because it isn't a failure.

**Loss — `alu_8bit`/gemma3:4b**, a diagnostic-flagged cell (Task 2.5 showed
STUCK_THRESHOLD rescuing this exact cell when toggled on):

| condition | pass | iters | stuck_fires |
|---|---|---|---|
| baseline | 3/3 | [3,3,3] | [1,1,1] |
| sem_adv | 3/3 | [3,3,3] | [1,1,1] |
| **sem_escalate** | **2/3** | [3,**F8**,3] | [1,**3**,1] |
| sem_escalate_restart | 3/3 | [3,3,3] | [1,1,1] |

`sem_escalate` loses a replicate that `baseline`, `sem_adv`, and
`sem_escalate_restart` all win cleanly. Replicate 2 escalates
(`stuck_threshold_fires=3`), STUCK_THRESHOLD fires inside the escalated
path, and it still fails at the iteration cap. This is surprising
specifically because STUCK_THRESHOLD toggled on was what *rescued* this
cell in Task 2.5 — here, in the escalated context, it doesn't. Flagged,
not absorbed: I do not have an explanation for why `sem_escalate_restart`
avoids this failure on the same cell with the same mechanism turned on;
it would need a targeted follow-up (not run here — one pass, no
re-runs) to say more. **That follow-up is deferred to Phase 2, not
forgotten** — a scoped-out item for the next phase, not an unchased loose
end in this one.

**Reading these four together:** 2 wins (1 real, 1 structural), 1 cost
with no pass/fail change, 1 loss. This is not a case for "`sem_escalate`
beats `sem_adv`" — it's a small, mixed sample with real movement in both
directions.

## §3. Cost result — the clean, separate headline

Mean heavyweight-tool invocations (Verilator + Yosys) per run, all 255
runs, all conditions (rough proxy: Verilator runs every iteration when
tools are on; exact yosys-skip-on-compile-failure accounting not
separately itemized here):

| condition | mean tokens/run | mean verilator+yosys calls/run |
|---|---|---|
| baseline | 4891 | 0.00 |
| raw_adv | 5354 | 2.61 |
| sem_adv | 4883 | 2.37 |
| **sem_escalate** | 5151 | **1.53** |
| **sem_escalate_restart** | 4719 | **1.37** |

**Two cost axes, and they diverge — both stay visible, neither is
hidden to make the story look cleaner.** `sem_escalate`'s mean token
count (5151) is *higher* than `sem_adv`'s (4883), not lower. That looks
like a contradiction of the efficiency claim below unless it's spelled
out: tool invocations and tokens are separate costs that move in
opposite directions here. `sem_escalate` reduces **heavyweight tool
invocations** (Verilator/Yosys subprocess spawns — the slow, external-
process part of the repair loop, and the specific axis this work
targets) by ~35-45%, but can *increase* token usage on cells where it
escalates and then burns extra iterations before passing — §2's
`counter_4bit` case (one replicate running to iteration 8) is the
visible instance of exactly that tradeoff. The efficiency claim below is
scoped to the tool-invocation axis, where it holds; it is not a claim
about token cost, which is the cheaper axis and moves the other way.
Report both, always — the token column stays in the table above rather
than being dropped or footnoted.

Escalation invokes heavyweight tools **~35-45% less often** than
unconditional `sem_adv`/`raw_adv`, while matching or exceeding their pass
rate on every category (see §4's aggregate table). **This is the actual
Phase 1 upgrade over the MLCAD paper's always-on `sem_adv` design: cost
and robustness, not pass-rate dominance.** Robustness in the specific
sense of §2's structural win — escalation is immune to the "tools hurt on
iteration 1" failure mode by construction, because it never applies tools
before there's evidence of a stuck failure. That is a real, defensible,
separate claim from "escalation wins on pass rate," and should be
presented as such rather than folded into a single headline number.

## §4. Regression gate — LOCKED comparison

LOCKED iteration cap: **6** (confirmed from `metrics_baseline.json` /
`metrics_raw_adv.json` / `metrics_sem_adv.json` in
`phase0/illustrations/proven/decoder_3to8/`, all `total_iterations: 6`) —
the same cap this run's `baseline`/`raw_adv`/`sem_adv` used, so those
three are a clean comparison. `sem_escalate`/`sem_escalate_restart` ran at
**Pass@8**, per the original Task 1 spec — **not** cap-equivalent to
LOCKED, flagged per cell below rather than treated as equivalent.

| cell | LOCKED (baseline / raw_adv / sem_adv) | this run, cap=6 | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| decoder_3to8/gemma3:4b | FAIL\|6 / FAIL\|6 / PASS\|2 | 0/3\|F6 / 0/3\|F6 / 3/3\|[2,2,2] | **PASS — exact replication** |
| decoder_3to8/llama3.1 | PASS\|2 / PASS\|2 / PASS\|2 | 3/3\|[2,2,2] / 3/3\|[2,2,2] / 3/3\|[2,2,2] | **PASS — exact replication** |
| mux4_oe/gemma3:4b | FAIL\|6 / FAIL\|6 / PASS\|2 | 0/3\|F6 / 0/3\|F6 / 3/3\|[2,2,2] | **PASS — exact replication** |
| mux4_oe/llama3.1 | PASS\|4 / PASS\|4 / PASS\|2 (efficiency regime: sem_adv fastest) | 3/3\|[4,4,4] / 3/3\|**[2,4,2]** / 3/3\|**[4,4,3]** | **PASS on pass/fail**; efficiency regime does **not** replicate |

**4/4 PASS on strict pass/fail** — nothing flips PASS↔FAIL relative to
LOCKED, at the cap LOCKED was captured at. One caveat flagged, not
absorbed: `mux4_oe`/llama3.1's "efficiency" characterization (sem_adv
fastest) does not hold in this fresh sample — `raw_adv` sometimes beats
`sem_adv` on iteration count. Nobody's pass/fail outcome changed, but the
specific illustrative property LOCKED was chosen to demonstrate did not
reproduce for this one sub-case.

`sem_escalate`/`sem_escalate_restart` pass all 4 regression-gate cells
(3/3 each) but at iteration 4 where `sem_adv` needs 2, on every cell where
escalation fires (all three gemma3:4b-affected cells: `escalated=True`,
`esc_iter=2` every time). Not directly comparable to LOCKED's cap=6
reference, but consistent with §3: escalation costs iterations when it
engages, in exchange for not needing tools on iterations where it
doesn't.

Do not read the pooled column as "escalation wins." It is dominated by the
regression gate (where `sem_adv` and both escalation conditions all hit

recovery — not by a broad advantage across real-test cells, most of which
showed no differentiation because §1's premise collapse means they never
got stuck long enough to test anything. The pooled number also actively
hides a loss: `sem_escalate`'s enabling-set rate (55.6%) is *lower* than
every other condition's, entirely because of `alu_8bit`/gemma3:4b (§2).

## Verdict

`sem_escalate` reads as a cost/robustness upgrade over the MLCAD paper's
always-on `sem_adv` design — same-or-better pass rate at roughly 35-45%
lower heavyweight-tool cost, and structurally immune to the
iteration-1-tools-hurt failure mode that cost `sem_adv` a clean win on
`ripple_carry_adder` — bought at the cost of extra iterations on the
cells where it does fire, and one clean replicate-level loss on a
diagnostic-flagged cell that isn't yet explained. It is not a pass-rate
win on the merits alone: most of the pre-registered run set didn't
replicate at N=3, the surviving evidence is a small four-cell mixed
sample (two wins, one cost, one loss), and the regression gate holds on
pass/fail but not on every illustrative property LOCKED was built to
show. That is the honest size of this result — cost and robustness,
demonstrated; broad pass-rate superiority, not demonstrated and not
claimed.
